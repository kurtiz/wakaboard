# Coding Week visual philosophy

## Measured warmth

The share image is a personal record made visible. Its first impression comes from the scale of one true number, then a small rhythm of seven days. Space gives that number room to feel earned. Every mark corresponds to data; the composition carries the emotion without invented achievement language.

WakaBoard's pine, emerald, amber, and warm ivory form a family rather than a uniform skin. Editorial uses the restraint of a printed field note. Night uses concentrated light against pine. Rhythm uses broad color fields and a more expressive beat. All lettering uses Outfit or Nunito. The three designs must look related when seen together and distinct enough that choosing one feels meaningful.

Typography provides the hierarchy: the duration is the visual anchor, the date range and category establish context, and supporting facts sit close to the chart they explain. Labels remain legible in a social feed. The design never relies on a tiny disclaimer to correct an ambiguous number. The period must be explicit, and the seven bars must show the actual daily distribution.

The chart is a record of effort across a week, including the quiet day. A zero day appears honestly as an empty mark. The high day is visible before its label is read. Each chart's bars are proportional to the same maximum and use a restrained shared visual grammar. Fine alignment and careful spacing matter more than added decoration.

These are export images, not pictures of app screens. No button, phone frame, tab bar, or share icon appears inside the art. The footer signs the image lightly. The project name is a removable supporting fact so private work can stay private without breaking the composition.
